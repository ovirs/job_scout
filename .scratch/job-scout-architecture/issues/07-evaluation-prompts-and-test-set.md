# What do the evaluation and extraction prompts say, and when is the test set trusted?

Type: prototype
Status: resolved (2026-10-14)

## Question

The LLM evaluation ticket fixed the shape of an Evaluation and left its wording open; the Application workflow ticket has now fixed the Triage values the test set is labelled with (Interested, Maybe, Ignored).

- What do the evaluation and extraction prompts say: the description of each score level from 1 to 10, the wording of the three recommendation levels so they line up with Interested, Maybe and Ignored, and the instructions for Deal Breaker verdicts and flags?
- How many triaged postings, and how many of each Triage value, must the test set hold before its agreement figure is trusted? Few Ignored labels will exist on postings the LLM skipped unless the user triages some from the skip list.
- How is agreement reported: per recommendation level, and how is the score's ordering compared with three Triage values?

## Answer

The prompts are drafted and the main choices in them settled; their exact wording is a baseline to be tuned once there are Triages to check it against. Phase 1 checks Evaluations by reading them, and figures are trusted only once the ETL brings enough postings.

- **Prompt drafts**: the evaluation and extraction prompts, with their output schemas, are on the throwaway branch `prototype/evaluation-prompts`, under `.scratch/job-scout-architecture/prototypes/07-evaluation-prompts/`, as primary sources. The evaluation call puts the instructions and the three inputs first (cached) and the posting last; the posting is treated as untrusted text.
- **Deal Breaker verdicts**: clear, violated or unknown ("met" is renamed, as it read backwards for a Deal Breaker worded as what disqualifies). A Deal Breaker is written however reads naturally; the LLM reads it for intent and applies it exactly as worded, no more strictly and no less. Its wording names the signals that count: "The role expects Spark experience: Spark is required, listed in the team's stack, or central to the daily work, including Spark-based platforms" is violated by a stack list or Databricks, not by "nice to have: Spark". Violated needs a quote; silence or real doubt is unknown. One kind of Deal Breaker only.
- **Recommendation**: apply (would want to apply this week), maybe (would keep it for later), skip (would not spend time on it), worded as a prediction of what the user would do, so it lines up with Interested, Maybe and Ignored. A violated Deal Breaker always means skip in the LLM's own recommendation too, with no exception for marginal cases; the code gate stays as a safety net. The recommendation is not derived from the score; apply is usually 7 or more and skip 4 or less.
- **Score**: credibility dominates. It is judged as a recruiter at the first screen in a crowded market, where adjacent experience is not a match; 7 or more needs every core requirement shown directly in the Career Profile. The Preferences move the score by a level or two at most, and a role they say to avoid scores 4 or less. The score ignores Deal Breakers. Most postings should land between 3 and 8. A hiring-manager framing is the first alternative to try if Interested postings keep scoring low.
- **Flags**: at most five red and five green, each one sentence specific to the user with a quote from the posting; nothing true of most postings and nothing a verdict already covers.
- **Additions to the Evaluation**: a one or two sentence summary for the inbox row; every quoted piece of evidence is checked in code to occur in the posting (ignoring whitespace, case, quote styles and bullets), else the result is malformed, retried and then left "not evaluated", and the posting view can highlight the quoted sentence.
- **Extraction**: only what the posting states, never inferred. Salary ranges and seniority levels are lists (pay per region, "Senior/Staff"); years of experience are not turned into a level; technologies are named ones only, required or nice-to-have, with "X, Y or Z" kept as alternatives. A manual entry's description is cut from the pasted text by first and last line, never retyped.
- **When the test set is trusted**: below 30 Triages or 8 of any value, too small to read (no headline figure); from 30 with 8 of each, indicative; from 100 with 20 of each, trusted. Even at 100, agreement is known only to about 9 points either side, and re-running the same prompt flips several postings, so two prompts are compared only by the postings that changed, and a difference counts as real only when the split is too lopsided for chance; otherwise the user reads the flipped postings.
- **Phase 1**: manual entries are biased towards Interested and Maybe and will not reach the thresholds. Each Evaluation is read instead, since a wrong verdict, flag or score is visible in a single case, and the user pastes 8 to 10 postings they would reject, some breaking a Deal Breaker, to check skip and the gate. Statistical tuning waits for the ETL.
- **Ignored labels after the ETL**: a "show me 10 random skipped postings" action, so Ignored labels are a fair sample of what the LLM skips. Seeing the recommendation before triaging inflates agreement; this is a known limit and nothing is built for it.
- **The report**: a table of Triage against recommendation; the share that agree with its range; the costly misses listed by posting (Interested but skipped, Ignored but apply); per recommendation level and per Triage, how often they match; score ordering as how often, of two postings triaged differently, the higher-triaged one has the higher score (ties count half), overall and per pair of values, with the spread of scores per Triage and the Triages of the top ten; the Deal Breaker gate, listing disqualified postings the user marked Interested; and, for two runs, the postings fixed and broken. A [demo of the report](../prototypes/07-evaluation-prompts/agreement-report.html) with invented data is on the same branch.

Rejected: an exception letting the LLM recommend a posting despite a violated Deal Breaker (the user wants a violation to mean skip; nuance belongs in the wording); a second "skill gap" kind of Deal Breaker judged by inference (a disqualification should rest on words the user can check); a writing rule fixing each Deal Breaker's polarity; a hiring-manager framing as the baseline (more forgiving of adjacent experience than the first screen is); the score weighing Preferences as much as credibility; trusting figures from a phase 1 test set of manual entries.
